What the operation involves: fat, gland or both

Gynecomastia is growth of the breast gland in boys and men, on one side or both. Surgery takes out fat, gland or both, and the plan depends on what an examination finds.

Your surgeon may suggest liposuction only or gland removal, or the two together. Liposuction reaches the fat through tiny cuts, and some surgeons use ultrasound- or power-assisted tools for it. Gland tissue is firm and has to be cut out, usually through a small cut at the edge of the areola or the side of the chest. Larger cases also need skin removal or tightening. The grades of gynecomastia help the surgeon choose.

Ask the surgeon

Is mine gland, fat or both, and what would you do about each part?

How the operation is done
  • Liposuction: fat is suctioned out through tiny cuts. A few very small marks.
  • Gland removal: the gland is cut out through a small cut at the edge of the areola or the side of the chest. One small line.
  • Skin removal or tightening: extra skin is cut away, or tightened with radiofrequency energy in some cases. Longer scars if skin is cut away.
  • Smaller operations may use local anaesthesia with sedation, larger ones general anaesthesia. Many are day care; some need one night. A thin drain is sometimes placed for a day or two.

Who it suits

Health
Good general health, a weight that has been steady for some months, and no smoking if you can manage it.
Expectations
A clear idea of what surgery can change. Scars remain and the result depends on your body.
Reason
The chest bothers you in daily life: clothes, the gym, swimming, or how you feel about it.
Who asks
Teenagers and their parents, bodybuilders, men in desk jobs, and older men with loose skin. Each group has a different first question, and the surgeon's answer differs too.

Is it fat or gland? When surgery is not the first step

The two look alike in a mirror and feel different to the hand. Only a doctor can tell after an examination, and may ask for an ultrasound or blood tests.

Hormone balance, some medicines and recent weight change can all cause chest enlargement, so see a doctor before you plan surgery. Read about fat or gland and chest fat without gland. Many teenagers settle without an operation, and some possible causes can be treated first. For what exercise can change, see our short guide.

Term

Pseudogynecomastia is chest fat that looks like gland. Many men have a mix of the two.

A hard lump, one side only, pain or nipple discharge. Do this before you plan surgery.
Possible causes
  • Hormone balance: relatively more oestrogen than testosterone, as in puberty, later life or some illnesses.
  • Some medicines, for example certain antibiotics, anxiety medicines and treatments for the prostate or the stomach.
  • Alcohol, anabolic steroids and some drugs.
  • Medical conditions such as kidney, liver or thyroid problems.
Signs to watch for
  • A lump or firm disc behind the nipple, on one or both sides.
  • Swelling that is uneven between the two sides.
  • Tenderness, or a wider areola.
  • Fluid from the nipple. This needs a doctor's check soon.
Myths: what does not remove gland
  • Creams, oils, homeopathy and fat-burner supplements: there is no good evidence that any of them removes gland tissue.
  • Chest exercise: it builds muscle under the tissue and can make a gland look more prominent, not smaller.
  • "It is always just fat": not so. Many men have gland, and gland does not shrink with diet.

Call the hospital the same day for fever, spreading redness, fast swelling on one side, bleeding through the dressing or severe pain. Chest pain or breathlessness: call 112.

The surgeon may ask you to wait
  • Your chest is still changing, or it started recently.
  • You use steroids or testosterone products. Stopping may help.
  • A liver, kidney or thyroid problem has not been looked at.
  • You plan to lose a lot of weight soon, or you smoke.
Tell the surgeon at the first visit
  • Every medicine and supplement, including gym supplements.
  • Alcohol and cannabis use.
  • When the enlargement began, and whether it changed in the last year.

Answers to 14 common questions

What is gynecomastia?

It is growth of breast gland tissue in boys and men, on one side or both. The chest can also look full because of fat alone. Doctors call that pseudogynecomastia, and many men have a mix of the two.

What are the signs, and which need a doctor soon?

A firm disc behind the nipple, uneven swelling, tenderness and a wider areola are the usual signs. A hard lump, change on one side only, pain or fluid from the nipple should be seen by a doctor early, before you think about surgery.

Who is a suitable candidate for surgery?

Surgeons look for good general health, a steady weight, a chest that has stopped changing, and a reason that matters to you. Smokers are usually asked to stop. A surgeon decides after an examination, not from a photo.

Is gynecomastia surgery done under general or local anaesthesia?

Smaller operations can be done under local anaesthesia with sedation. Larger operations, or gland removal together with liposuction, usually use general anaesthesia. The surgeon and the anaesthetist decide after your examination and a fitness check.

How many days off work do I need?

Many people return to desk work after about a week, and some need longer. Driving is usually after about two weeks and chest training after six weeks or more. These are typical timings, not promises, and your surgeon sets yours. The recovery guide shows the full pattern week by week.

Will I have visible scars?

Yes, some mark remains. For gland removal the cut is usually small and sits at the edge of the areola, where the skin colour changes, or at the side of the chest. Liposuction leaves tiny marks. Larger cases with loose skin may need longer scars. Scars start red and usually fade over a year or more. Ask the surgeon to mark the planned cuts on your chest.

Can I go home to Delhi, Noida or Faridabad the same day?

After a day-care operation many people go home the same day, but you should not drive yourself. Bring an attendant, and plan the ride so you are not stuck in traffic for hours on day one. The surgeon and the anaesthetist decide whether you stay a night.

Does insurance cover gynecomastia surgery?

Usually not, because most policies treat it as cosmetic. Some may consider it when a doctor records a medical need. Ask your insurer in writing before you book, and ask the hospital which diagnosis and procedure it will put on the estimate.

Gurgaon or Gurugram: is it the same place?

Yes. Gurgaon and Gurugram are the same city in Haryana, and both spellings are in common use. This page uses Gurgaon because more people search for it.

What kind of place is the operation done in?

A partner day-care surgical centre or a hospital, depending on the plan. We do not name it on this page. The checks listed above apply to either.

Does the heat of a Gurgaon summer matter for recovery?

It can. A compression vest is worn under clothes for weeks, and it is uncomfortable in heat. Ask for a breathable garment and a spare, and plan the first week indoors with air conditioning if you can.

Can the enlargement come back?

It can. Weight gain, anabolic steroids, some medicines, alcohol and some medical conditions can cause new enlargement. Read about the possible causes, and if the cause is still there, tell the surgeon before you decide.

Can creams, oils or supplements remove it?

There is no good evidence that creams, oils, homeopathy or fat-burner supplements remove gland tissue. Fat may shrink with weight loss, gland usually does not. Chest exercise builds muscle and can make a gland look more prominent.

What if I need a second operation?

It happens. Ask the hospital, in writing, whether a second operation is charged and for how long after the first. You can compare two quotes on exactly this line.
